Speed & Motion

Auto: reacts to motion but is a black box — GoPro doesn't publish exactly how it decides. Tends to run near 30x for biking, which compresses too aggressively and causes missed moments.

Fixed (10x/15x): total predictability — you know exactly what you're getting every time. Trade-off: no automatic adjustment for pace changes.

Benefit of fixed: removes guesswork entirely. Cost: none that matters for riding.

Drops TimeWarp down to real-speed (1x) or half-speed on a tap, then resumes on a second tap. Best triggered by the front Mode button (assign via Settings → Shortcuts) rather than the touchscreen — easier to hit blind while riding.

Use opportunistically on unfamiliar trails, planned in advance on known routes. Hold 5–8 sec for scenery, 10–15 sec for something you'll trim down later (bridges, buildings).

Benefit: gives a moment real screen time. Cost: requires a physical tap — skip it if riding demands full attention.
Image & Stabilization

Digital stabilization. AutoBoost starts as wide as standard HyperSmooth and only crops in tighter when it detects a rough section — then backs back out. Standard HyperSmooth applies a fixed crop all the time, whether needed or not.

Benefit: widest FOV most of the time, extra smoothing exactly when needed. No real cost — always the better pick for biking.
2.7K (4:3)Taller FOV, less drain
4K (16:9 only)More detail, crop room

4:3 only exists at 2.7K/1440p — 4K forces a wider 16:9 crop, less ideal for POV mounts. 4K also uses more battery/heat/storage.

Benefit of 2.7K: better suited to POV mounts, lighter on the camera. Use 4K only for planned shoots needing post-crop flexibility.

Wide/Superwide: reads as faster, more immersive — default for riding.

Linear: more stable, no distortion — better for architecture with strong vertical lines (avoids bent verticals that Wide introduces).

Trade-off: Wide feels faster but bends straight lines; Linear is accurate but loses the sense of speed.
Night & Timelapse
5 secSmoother, more editable
10 secMore compression, jumpier

5-sec is the safer default — more captured frames means more room to trim/adjust pacing in post. Reserve 10-sec+ for very slow, still subjects (calm sky, minimal cloud motion) where you're deliberately compressing hours into a short clip.

Benefit of 5-sec: real editing flexibility. Cost: more storage, longer real-time shoot for the same output length.
Reliability

Single button press starts recording immediately from off — no menu navigation, no mode-then-shutter sequence. Run it per-ride, not per-scene.

Benefit: removes the "did that actually register" uncertainty — one motion, one button.

Beep and front screen exist, but aren't realistic to check mid-ride at speed. Trust QuikCapture — the failure mode is usually a missed press, not a recording error. Do any real check at a natural stop, not while moving.

Benefit of trusting it: removes a genuine safety risk (checking a light while riding). Real safety net is a post-ride card check.
