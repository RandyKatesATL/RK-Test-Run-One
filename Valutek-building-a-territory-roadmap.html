Field Reference — Territory Planning

Building Target Accounts With Data, Not Gut Feel

Your reps already have instincts about who's worth chasing. This roadmap doesn't replace that — it gives them proof, priority, and a plan they can defend in the field.

01 — Process
1
Foundation

Define the ICP

+
ObjectiveName the traits of a customer that buys fast, buys often, and doesn't churn — before touching a single account list.
Key Questions For The Team
  • What 4–6 firmographic traits define our best customers — and can you name why each one matters?
  • Which industry/NAICS codes actually convert, versus just sound relevant?
  • Where's the revenue and employee-count sweet spot — and where do we consistently lose deals outside it?
  • What facility type or regulatory tier (cleanroom class, GMP status) predicts a real buyer?
  • Is purchasing centralized or plant-by-plant — and does that change who we target first?
Data Inputs
CRM closed-won historyChurn analysisWin/loss notes
2
Universe

Build the Territory Universe

+
ObjectivePull every account in the territory that fits the ICP filters — the full "TAM in my backyard," not the list a rep already knows.
Key Questions For The Team
  • Which data source defines "every account that fits" — D&B, ZoomInfo, trade association rosters?
  • How many accounts in this territory actually match the ICP filters from Step 1?
  • Does this list confirm what's already in CRM, or does it expose gaps we didn't know existed?
  • When a rep's mental list disagrees with the data pull — is that a coaching conversation or a data problem?
Data Inputs
D&B / ZoomInfo pullNAICS filterGeographic radiusTrade association directories
3
Enrichment

Layer In Whitespace & Signal Data

+
ObjectiveTurn a static list into a living one by identifying who has no spend with us today, and who's showing signs of buying soon.
Key Questions For The Team
  • Which ICP-fit accounts have zero dollars with us right now (whitespace)?
  • What intent signals are visible — hiring surges, facility expansion, new equipment, permit filings?
  • What's changed in this account's world in the last 90 days — M&A, new plant, leadership change, regulatory shift?
  • Which signals can we actually detect today, and which need a new data source we don't have yet?
Data Inputs
CRM gap analysisZoomInfo intent dataPermit databasesLinkedIn leadership changesTrade press

Key Questions For The Team
  • What weight does each fit criterion get — and is that weight based on win-rate data or opinion?
  • Where's our actual cutoff between A, B, and C — and will two different reps land on the same tier for the same account?
  • Are sales and marketing scoring accounts the same way, or running two different formulas?
5
Ownership

Assign Ownership & Build Account Plans

+
ObjectiveTurn the tiered list into a plan a rep can execute Monday morning — and can explain in their own words.
Key Questions For The Team
  • Who owns each Tier-A account, and do they know it?
  • For your top 5 accounts — who's the economic buyer, what's the trigger, what's the entry-point offer?
  • Can you present your top 5 back to me, in your own words, with a hypothesis for "why now"?
Data Inputs
Org charts / LinkedInCRM contact rolesRep-built account plans
6
Discipline

Build the Refresh Cadence

+
ObjectiveA target list decays the moment it's finished. This step is what turns it into a system instead of a one-time project.
Key Questions For The Team
  • On what cadence do we re-score — quarterly, at minimum?
  • What specifically triggers a tier promotion or demotion mid-cycle?
  • What's our rule for retiring a dead account instead of letting it sit on the list forever?
Data Inputs
Updated CRM activityRefreshed firmographic pullRefreshed signal data
The Coaching Move

Don't hand your team the finished list. Walk one rep through Steps 1–4 live, using their own territory. Then have them teach it to the next rep. A list you hand down gets used once. A method someone builds themselves gets used every quarter.
