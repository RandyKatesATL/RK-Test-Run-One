The Core Bet

Connection, consistency, technical credibility

What made this model work in the old business wasn't a tactic, it was a formula. A Fastenal seller with a cleanroom question calls the person who already knows them, always picks up, and actually knows the answer, not the vendor who emailed once in March. Every contact, whether a seller or a branch, moves through the same three stages. The role's calendar is a blend of pushing new contacts through Stage 1, maintaining Stage 2, and staying responsive to Stage 3.

1

Connection

One real conversation, no pitch. Learn their world. Plant the flag: call or text me when cleanroom comes up.

→
2

Rhythm

A predictable recurring touch, spaced widely enough to respect their time, tight enough to never be forgotten.

→
3

Embedded Resource

They call first. Scheduled cadence loosens because real engagement now happens on their timeline, not ours.

◆

Why this matters for every bucket belowStage migration, the share of a book that's moved cold → Stage 1 → Stage 2 → Stage 3, is the one number that tells you the model is working. Not call volume. Not activity. Whether the phone is starting to ring the other direction.

Master Workplan

The four work buckets

These stack, they don't run in parallel from day one. Early on, it's almost entirely Bucket 1. By month twelve, all four are running, and the rep is operating with real autonomy. Click into each to see the 90-day and year-one markers, and the strategic questions Andi needs to answer before execution.

1

Seller Engagement

Trust, cadence, technical reflex · dominates Months 1–4
90 days

Every front-line seller in the initial book (~70 names, CSC first) has had a real first conversation, no pitch. The flag is planted: call or text me. A working cadence tracker exists so nothing depends on memory.

Year one

A meaningful share of the book has crossed into Stage 3, sellers calling first. That's the number that proves the model, not call volume.

1

What's the real weekly call capacity once the base passes 40–50 active relationships, and what happens when that ceiling is hit, slow the pipeline or bring on help sooner?

2

What's the actual escalation path when a rep gets a technical question they can't answer, and how fast does the answer need to come back to protect the credibility being built?

3

How do we know a contact has genuinely moved from cold to connected, versus just been called once? What's the real signal, not the checkbox?

2

Branch Penetration

Data-driven expansion, the family ladder · dominates Months 3–9
90 days

First-wave prioritized branches, real spend and real cleanroom-eligible accounts but shallow penetration, have at least one seller relationship at Stage 2. No branch profile ever gets delivered cold, that's the gate.

Year one

A real cohort of branches is visibly climbing the ladder, one product family to two, two to three, with named accounts behind each rung.

1

What's the actual disqualifying line for a branch, spend threshold, geography, account density, so hours aren't spent chasing branches that were never going to move?

2

Who owns the decision when a branch has a great opportunity number but no relationship yet? Hold the reveal, or fast-track it?

3

If a branch stalls at two families instead of climbing to three, is that a relationship problem, a data problem, or just normal pace, and who's checking?

3

Campaigns & Incentives

Fuel that accelerates 1 and 2 · ongoing, layered in as relationships mature
90 days

The first small, fast seller-level campaign is live, something like a First Call Bonus, rewarding the act of reaching out, not a sale. Simple enough to run without heavy tracking overhead.

Year one

Campaigns are matched to specific ladder rungs and relationship stages. At least one branch-level offer, like free drop-ship on a second family, has a proven, repeatable conversion story.

1

Who approves and funds these offers as they scale? What's the real budget ceiling before this needs formal sign-off instead of ad hoc approval?

2

How do we stop a campaign from landing on a branch or seller that isn't ready for it? What's the actual checkpoint before an offer goes out?

3

What's the plan if a campaign works too well, more uptake than fulfillment (samples, drop-ship logistics, quotes) can actually support?

4

Special Projects & Personal Development

Autonomy, strategic thinking, career path · Months 6–12 and beyond
90 days

The rep is still fully inside Book 1, the distributor book, building the muscle. No special projects yet, that's intentional. This bucket is earned, not assigned on day one.

Year one

The rep has meaningfully touched Book 2 (their own end-user outbound) and possibly Book 3 (filtering a strategic but unproven opportunity list). This is where the role visibly becomes a career.

1

What's the actual readiness bar for moving a rep from Book 1 into Book 2, tenure-based, performance-based, or a conversation, and who makes that call?

2

If a rep uncovers something genuinely valuable in Book 3 research, what's the path for that insight to reach you and Randy fast enough to matter?

3

How do we keep Book 1 from suffering once a rep starts splitting time across books? What's the guardrail that protects the core relationship work?

●

The unit of measure this whole national conversation hangs onAt full maturity, one dedicated, fully ramped rep can realistically own and sustain one region's Tier A book — roughly 73–80 people. That's the ceiling, not the starting headcount, and it turns "how many reps do we eventually need" into a real answer: up to one per region, twelve at full national scale.

Validate before scaling further

1

Does the 8–10 calls/week pace hold once the rep is also handling real reactive Stage 3 support, or does it need to come down as the book matures?

2

What's the real stage-migration rate, cold → Stage 1 → Stage 2, inside the 90-day window? This number makes or breaks the case for regions two through twelve.

3

How much of the maintenance load can eventually be absorbed by branch-level campaign work, since an actively-expanding branch may need less scheduled maintenance and more reactive support?

Validate before scaling further

1

Does the quarterly review cadence hold once a branch relationship deepens, or do the fastest-climbing branches need a tighter cadence than ones just holding steady?

2

When capacity is tight, does the rep protect Phase 1–2 (revenue today) or push into Phase 3 (long-term upside)? That trade-off needs a stated answer, not a moment-by-moment judgment call.

3

Once a region's combined book hits the capacity ceiling, what's the actual trigger for a second rep, a number, a stalled growth rate, or judgment, and who owns making that call?

Appendix C

Special Projects Slate

The menu, not the mandate. One project at a time, chosen from this list once a rep clears the Bucket 4 readiness bar. Two Book 2 projects build a rep's own outbound motion; three Book 3 projects sharpen strategic judgment by turning a hunch into a ranked answer.

Book 2 · End-User Target Projects

Southwest Data Centers

60–90 days to first warm engagements

Build and warm a target list of data center operators; identify contamination-control needs tied to server manufacturing and maintenance.

  • How many real named targets exist, 15 or 150?
  • Who's the right first contact, facilities or procurement?
  • Does an existing distributor already touch these accounts?

Large-Format Digital Printers

60–90 days to first warm engagements

Same motion, applied to print operations where dust and particulate control affect output quality.

  • Is contamination control even a recognized pain point here?
  • What's realistic account size vs. semiconductor or med device?
  • Is there a natural distributor already serving this vertical?

Book 3 · Market Research / New Biz Projects

CHIPS Act-Funded Sites

4–6 weeks to a ranked recommendation

Separate genuine near-term cleanroom demand from sites still years from operational.

  • Which sites are breaking ground vs. still in permitting limbo?
  • Who's already selling in, incumbent or true whitespace?
  • Realistic entry point, direct, Fastenal, or another distributor?

Nanofabs

4–6 weeks to a ranked recommendation

University and private nanofab facilities as a distinct, smaller-scale segment.

  • Recurring consumables business, or one-time buildout spend?
  • How many nanofabs actually exist at a worthwhile scale?
  • Does this connect to the GCU relationship already in motion?

Universities with Cleanrooms

4–6 weeks to a ranked recommendation

Builds on the UGIM insight that university procurement often runs through small regional distributors.

  • Does this validate a "meet them where they buy" strategy?
  • Which regional distributors keep showing up across schools?
  • Revenue play, or long-term brand pipeline with future buyers?
Thought-Partner Review

Before this goes live

The strategy is right, the sequencing logic and stage-gate thinking is genuinely differentiated. These are the honest notes on what to tighten, what still needs real answers, and what would raise confidence before month one.

Polish

Add up the hours in one placeEvery appendix states its own call ceiling, but none account for CRM documentation, training delivery, trade shows, and RFQ support the role profile also asks for. Build one real 40-hour budget before launch.
Name a pilot region with a head startDon't start from a national average. Southeast already shows a doubled branch run rate and 60 new branches, real momentum, not a hypothesis.
Turn stage gates into numbers"Is the relationship real enough" is currently a feeling. For a first-time manager, that's exactly where judgment freezes under pressure. Convert the biggest gates into explicit thresholds.

Still uncomfortable with

Capacity math under real reactive loadThe 25–26 call ceiling assumes scheduled, proactive calls. It doesn't yet account for unscheduled, same-day RFQ and cross-reference support, the actual Book 1 promise. Untested until real inbound volume hits.
Hunter and expert, same six monthsThe role profile says months 1–6 are for learning the business. This plan has Waves 1–3 and Branch Phase 1 largely landing in that same window. Real tension, not yet resolved.

Raises confidence

A tracker exists before day oneOne row per seller, one per branch: stage, last touch, next touch due. Without it, stage migration never gets measured.
A named campaign approver and budget envelopeSet the ceiling once, let the rep and Andi operate inside it.
A hard 30/60/90 recalibration checkpointNot a review of the rep, a review of whether the assumptions in these appendices held up against reality.
