Key Assumptions

What this whole framework is built on. Worth a glance if the room feels different than expected — check these first.

FORMAT

  • 40 minutes of content, 20 minutes of table talk and Q&A — no open-floor Q&A for a room this size.
  • Randy's call: table-based discussion beats individual Q&A in a large, culturally mixed room where hands don't go up easily.
  • Reused in Japan in November — build it once, refine it twice.

AUDIENCE

  • Mostly directors, managers, and engineers. Not a purchasing-heavy room.
  • Mixed semiconductor and medical device leaders in the same room — different ISO classes, same underlying human problem.
  • Three other speakers on the agenda: standards/science (Hasim), cleanroom performance (Chong Loc), disinfectants (Lee Lim). Greg's talk sits at the most strategic, least technical altitude of the four — that's a strength, not a gap.

POSITIONING PHILOSOPHY

  • Data smooths and shapes the story. It doesn't replace it. Every stat in this deck should serve a human point, never stand alone.
  • Balance test for every regional insight: "I understand your industry challenges" AND "I know your conditions are genuinely different" — never lecture the room on their own culture back to them.
  • Leadership behavior is the lever throughout, not operator behavior. Every story should leave leaders feeling capable of acting, not blamed.

STILL OPEN — GREG'S HOMEWORK

  • Which single story (cardboard/tape vs. bunny-suit) does he tell with the most conviction?
  • How directly is he willing to tell leaders their own instinct might be the risk?
  • Does the Southeast Asia material stay general, or get specific to this room?
  • What's the actual visual for the newsletter concept — real mockup or verbal only?
  • Confirm the annotated Venn diagram as the table-talk artifact, or simplify further.
  • The big one: what's the one sentence a manager who's never met him carries back to their team on Monday?

Two distinct tiers feed this workforce: a scarce university-trained engineering tier, and a much larger TVET-pathway operator/technician tier, many tracked into the industry since their teens. A meaningful share of hands actually touching product are non-local and multilingual.

Gen Z finding, worth having in your back pocket This generation isn't resistant to rigor — they're resistant to rigor that feels arbitrary. Purpose-based framing (who receives this device, who uses this chip) lands harder than compliance framing.
HANDLE WITH CARE Power Distance Index for Malaysia sits near the top of any country ever measured — employees are culturally conditioned not to escalate even when something looks wrong. This is real and useful, but it never gets named as a stat or index on a slide. It lives only inside Section 6, reframed as a leadership-posture point, never a culture lecture. Do not put this number in front of the room.

Full Detail

01Fortunate to Be Together — Let's Make Every Touch Today Matter+
CORE IDEA
25 years invested in becoming the person leaders call when they're stuck, not the person who writes the standard.
KEY CONCEPT
Credibility through relationships and problem-solving, not credentials.
VISUAL / DATA
None. Runs on presence, not proof.
02Capital Isn't Your Constraint — Optimizing the Human Side of Execution Is+
CORE IDEA
The gap between world-class and average isn't decided in strategy meetings — it's decided in thousands of small physical moments on the floor.
KEY CONCEPT
Reframes the whole talk as a leadership opportunity before any story begins.
VISUAL / DATA
RM20.8B investment / 80% export stat card. One big number, small context line.
03Three Circles, One Missing Connection — You Hold the Bridge+
CORE IDEA
Technical experts, operators, and production management sit close together on the org chart, with almost no real connective tissue between them.
KEY CONCEPT
The Venn diagram is the mechanism the entire talk hangs on. Leadership owns the white space between the circles — that's an opportunity, not a shortfall. Optional line: QA measures if it was done right, 5S organizes the conditions to do it right, Every Touch Matters is what makes both stick when no one's watching.
VISUAL / DATA
The Venn diagram itself — animated build, circles appear one at a time, then the gap gets highlighted. The most important visual in the deck.
04The Why Behind the Rule — Turn Compliance into Conviction+
CORE IDEA
The cardboard/tape story — operators trying to do the right thing, improvising badly because the why was never connected to them.
KEY CONCEPT
Technical debt — a facility launches optimal and quietly erodes as headcount and equipment grow without matching discipline. Strongest leadership-exposure moment. Give it real weight.
VISUAL / DATA
Day One vs. Month 18 visual — same footprint, more people, less discipline. No external stat needed, runs on the story's own logic.
05A Different Floor, A Different Playbook — Lead Where You Actually Stand+
CORE IDEA
Contamination risk here compounds on top of a workforce reality most HQ playbooks weren't built for — and the leaders who see that clearly get ahead of it.
KEY CONCEPT
Victor won cooperation with a financial case, not a directive — the only lever that scales when tenure and shared language can't be counted on.
VISUAL / DATA
4-stat regional reality panel: 50,000 engineer shortfall · 14-60% turnover · 15% brain drain · 2.5M migrant workforce. Icon-based layout, not a chart — this is about scale and humanity.
06Your Posture, Not Their Culture — The Next Level of Leadership Starts Here+
CORE IDEA
The person on the line today may be new, multilingual, and less likely to flag a problem even when they see one. Not a workforce weakness — an invitation for leadership to adapt.
KEY CONCEPT
Leadership behavior is the variable that's actually in the room's control.
VISUAL / DATA
None. Spoken bridge only, clean mostly-text slide. Never a stat or index — see the Data tab warning.
07Small Materials, Big Signal — What Your Gloves Say About Your People+
CORE IDEA
Consumables look interchangeable on a spec sheet, but investing there is a leadership signal, not a procurement footnote.
KEY CONCEPT
Optional third story. Ties resource-allocation discipline to something the whole room touches daily.
VISUAL / DATA
Simple cost comparison, premium consumable vs. average cost of a contamination event (napkin math). Flag: closest section to sounding like a pitch — principle-level only, no brand, no product.
08Close the Gap in Plain Sight — Make the Invisible Visible+
CORE IDEA
Leadership-sponsored, visual, recurring — real fingerprints and skin flakes on parts, not theoretical language.
KEY CONCEPT
Answers the turnover problem directly. A visual message survives a shift change even when the trainer who built it doesn't. Proof the gap can close, not just a warning it exists.
VISUAL / DATA
Newsletter mockup — before/after part photos, one sentence of explanation. Visual callback to the Section 3 circles, now shown connected.
092.1 Million Chances a Year — Every One Is Yours to Win+
CORE IDEA
The single number that makes every touch matters viscerally real, right before the room takes it into their own hands.
KEY CONCEPT
Sets up the table exercise as a small-scale version of the cross-silo connection the talk has been building toward.
VISUAL / DATA
2.1M standalone, minimal text. Pair with: "Every one of them is a chance to get it right." Keep it positive — no "or wrong" framing.
10Your Table, Your Breakthrough — Learn From the Room+
CORE IDEA
Each table annotates a simple Venn diagram for their own organization, then compares notes — structured peer exchange, not open Q&A.
KEY CONCEPT
Directly activates the goal of leveraging other leaders' perspectives on shared challenges — the room becomes the resource.
VISUAL / DATA
Printed handout, blank three-circle Venn diagram per table, large enough to write on. No screen data needed — the exercise is the content.
11What Will You Carry Home — Make Monday Count+
CORE IDEA
One sentence Greg wants a manager who's never met him to bring back to their own team.
KEY CONCEPT
This is the actual deliverable of the entire talk. Everything before it is scaffolding to make this sentence land.
VISUAL / DATA
Still open — Greg's sentence to write. Once written, likely the final slide, alone, large text.

CUT — AND WHY

  • 70-80% process-vs-operator debate — better raised by a table than pre-litigated on stage.
  • Squeeze bottle/nitrogen gun story — redundant with the cardboard story.
  • Direct references to the other speakers' content inside the talk — useful for prep, not for the room.
  • Power Distance Index, named by number or label — the insight is used, never the stat itself.
