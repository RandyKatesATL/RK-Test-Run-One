How the Mat Works
Disposable polyethylene film sheets — each coated with pressure-sensitive adhesive. Dirt sticks. Peel the layer. Done.
01
Mat Tack Level
The "tack" is the stickiness — adhesive strength precisely balanced during manufacturing. Too weak and particles don't transfer from the shoe. Valutek engineers this balance for optimal capture across all mat sizes.
02
Adhesive Softness
If adhesive is too hard, particles bounce off instead of sticking. If too soft, adhesive transfers to the shoe or wheel — defeating the purpose. Valutek's formulation hits the window exactly.
03
Film & Adhesive Thickness
Adhesive layers run 2–4mm (50–100 microns). Too thin and particles stay on the surface, shortening useful mat life. Valutek's 0.05 micron film thickness is thicker than the competition — by design.

Critical Sizing Rule: Never Tile
Avoid placing two smaller mats side-by-side at a doorway entrance — this decreases performance by allowing particles through the joint. Always span the full entrance with one mat. When changing sheets, start at the tabbed corner (closest to the doorway) and roll the waste film into a ball as you go.

Data Centers Have One Existential Enemy: Heat.
And Heat's Best Friend Is Dust.

Strip away the servers and the software and a data center is a precision-controlled cleanroom facility — operating 24/7/365 with zero tolerance for downtime. A modern build runs 250,000 to 500,000+ square feet. Costs $100 million to $2 billion to construct. And the moment the construction crews leave, it gets plugged in and never closes.

Every particle of dust that enters and settles on a server, a cooling vent, or a subfloor plenum is an insulator. It traps heat. Heat forces cooling systems to work harder. Harder cooling means higher energy consumption — the single largest operating cost in the building.

Three Contamination Vectors
Threat Zone 1
Above the Floor
Airborne dust, skin cells, and clothing fibers settle into server cooling vents, act as thermal insulation, and force cooling systems to compensate — driving up energy costs continuously.
→ Shoe covers · Pre-sat wipers · PPE kit
Threat Zone 2
Below the Floor
The subfloor plenum is the primary airflow highway. Dust accumulation restricts airflow, degrades cooling performance, and creates hot spots that kill hardware — silently and expensively.
→ Pre-sat wipers · Cleaning program
Threat Zone 3 — YOUR ENTRY
At the Door
Every person who enters carries contamination. Without the right protocols at entry points, the room's cleanliness degrades with every shift change. This is the most controllable threat — and the easiest win.
